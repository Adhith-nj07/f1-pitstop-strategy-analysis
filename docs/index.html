LOG.01 — PIT LANE SPEED

Pit stops got faster. Then they stopped getting faster.

Median pit lane time fell from 22.4s in 2011 to a peak efficiency window around 2019, spiked briefly during COVID-disrupted 2020, then resumed its decline to roughly 23.1s by 2025 — teams optimized hard, hit a wall, and have only shaved off fractions since.

⚠ SYSTEM NOTE — SIGNAL DEFINITION "Duration" here means full pit lane time (entry to exit, ~20-25s), not the sub-3-second stationary wheel-change time quoted on TV broadcasts, which needs trackside light-beam timing this public dataset doesn't include. Pit lane time is still a fair, consistent yardstick for comparing teams and seasons.

Which teams are fastest today?

The fastest and slowest pit crews in the most recent full season differ by almost exactly one second in median stop time — a real, repeatable edge that has nothing to do with the car underneath.

LOG.02 — RACE STRATEGY

Fewer stops line up with better finishes — correlation, not proof of cause.

Across every race from 2011–2026, one-stop drivers finished 8.9th on average; four-stop races averaged 10.5th. The gap is real, but it cuts both ways: drivers already running near the front also need fewer reactive stops for damage or tire trouble.

LOG.03 — DRIVER CONSISTENCY

Every driver in the field is already remarkably consistent.

Measuring each driver's lap-time coefficient of variation — calculated within a single race, then averaged across the season, so a Monaco lap is never compared against a Spa lap — the entire grid sits inside a narrow 1.9%–2.3% band.

So does consistency win races?

Plotting each driver's consistency score against their average finishing position that season gives a strikingly flat relationship.

r = 0.05

Essentially no linear correlation between consistency and finishing position among current F1 drivers — a genuinely useful null result. It suggests consistency is a threshold skill — you need enough of it to race in Formula 1 at all — rather than a differentiator once you're already there. Car and team performance decide the rest.
